# GlobalLink NEXT USA — San Francisco 2026
## Post-event research and design review
Version 1.0 · Reviewed November 2, 2026 · Research-led, not an organiser-approved audit

## 1. Executive assessment
The public evidence now supports a real on-site conference, not merely a scheduled event: a San Francisco attendee reflection posted October 28 includes five photographs of welcome signage, a branded staircase, an agenda board, a digital-health panel in progress and event pins. A Verndale post on October 29 reflects on its Beyond the Hype panel contribution. Together these establish specific activity and useful themes; they do not establish total attendance, commercial return, programme completion or overall event satisfaction.

The event's strongest observable design quality is a connected arrival-to-room brand experience. The photographs show NEXT carried from a welcome banner to architectural stair graphics, an agenda pillar, room branding and small keepsakes. The principal review opportunity is not another visual theme: it is stronger evidence capture, legibility checks at real viewing distances, and a closed-loop connection between what was designed, what shipped and what people actually saw.

This is an initial, evidence-bounded review. No overall letter grade or numerical performance score is assigned. Five attendee photographs cannot support a whole-event design audit comparable to the larger London photo review.

## 2. Event identity and evidence method
Event: GlobalLink NEXT USA, hosted by TransPerfect. Scheduled October 27–28, 2026, InterContinental San Francisco. The official USA site uses GlobalLink NEXT; Element's event workspace uses TransPerfect NEXT. This report preserves both without treating the London edition as San Francisco evidence.

Research checked official event, agenda and speaker pages, TransPerfect's corporate event listing, CMSWire's calendar, public LinkedIn updates and targeted searches for partner, keynote, venue, Instagram and awards coverage. Official WordPress REST media and posts returned no public attachments/posts for October 27–November 2; that is an API/search gap, not evidence that no photographer archive exists.

Sources S1–S5 are scheduled/promotional baselines. S6 is an opening-day search-index statement with unsuccessful direct retrieval. S7 and S8 are directly retrieved post-event/onsite reflections with structured publication dates. Live retrieval is not an archived historical snapshot. Social engagement figures are omitted because they change and do not represent event attendance.

## 4. Programme and content review
The official programme is a planned baseline, not an as-delivered transcript. It includes technology spotlights, AI governance, localization, life sciences, digital experience and client case studies [S3]. Historical/current sponsor logos on the website are not proof that every displayed company participated in this edition.

Two supported content signals emerge:
1. Human experience alongside AI. Jessica's account highlights attentive hospitality, customer-journey touchpoints, individual recognition and giving teams permission to create thoughtful moments [S7]. These are her attributed takeaways, not a verbatim Guidara transcript.
2. Outcomes before tools. Verndale's retrospective advocates selecting workflows, defining measurable outcomes and making more valuable work possible [S8]. This is a partner viewpoint, not proof of realised ROI.

Recommended follow-up: develop one consent-cleared hospitality/customer-experience story and one workflow-to-outcome story, each approved by the speaker or source owner. Request delivered decks/recordings before publishing session summaries. Do not reuse the agenda's unsupported AI failure percentage as a research finding.

## 5. Photo-led design review
All five originals are private research references. Posted October 28 by Jessica Wanamaker; photographer and reuse permission unconfirmed. Event association comes from the post and matching signs, not independently verified capture timestamps. No image has been AI modified. Room assignment is intentionally unset.

### P1 — Welcome banner
Observed: a freestanding vertical GlobalLink NEXT WELCOME banner is visible beside glazing and a stair landing. NEXT branding is high on the face, with a large vertical welcome word below.
Strength: clear arrival cue and strong continuity with the event family.
Risk to inspect: pale gradient sections can lose separation against window light; one angled photograph cannot establish true colour or distant readability.
Action: compare a front-facing shot under actual daylight with the released master; evaluate welcome readability from both approach directions. Keep original brand geometry and approved inks.

### P2 — Branded staircase
Observed: NEXT lettering spans several blue stair risers, with GlobalLink above and TransPerfect attribution below. It is architectural branding, not a conventional single-panel sign.
Strength: a distinctive, immersive arrival moment that turns existing venue architecture into a large brand surface.
Risk to inspect: letter continuity and attribution depend on viewing position; stair tread/edge safety and material approval cannot be assessed from a photo.
Action: retain this as a high-impact arrival precedent, contingent on venue safety approval. Obtain frontal and approach-angle photographs, installation measurements and the issued artwork. Never infer physical dimensions from the shot.

### P3 — On-site agenda pillar
Observed: a freestanding agenda face carries Tuesday October 27 and Wednesday October 28 beneath a NEXT lockup. Session copy is considerably smaller than the date headings.
Strength: both days are consolidated in one recognisable branded display.
Risk to inspect: small agenda text and a darker corridor may limit stand-off reading. This downloaded image is not a reliable test of the original print's type sharpness.
Action: test at intended reading distance; prioritise day/time/session hierarchy, room names and mobile agenda access. Check scans using the original artwork; no QR performance claim is possible from this photograph.

### P4 — Digital health panel
Observed: four seated panelists, a screen with the digital-health session title, and NEXT side branding. Audience heads occupy the lower foreground; some right-side branding is visually interrupted by the seated speaker.
Strength: screen and side panels form a coherent room frame. The session title remains identifiable in this photograph.
Risk to inspect: audience/speaker blocking, screen brightness versus printed surround and side-logo height. One shot cannot yield a blocking percentage or all-room grade.
Action: compare frontal room and back-row photographs to the room plan and released screen-surround artwork. Carry forward the London upper-third logo and camera/sightline principles as a checklist, not as a confirmed SF defect across all rooms.

### P5 — Pins and memorabilia
Observed: a collection of small event pins/stickers photographed on fabric, including NEXT/GlobalLink and hospitality-themed items.
Strength: memorable, personal pieces extend the event beyond a screen or sign and fit the hospitality theme in the attendee's reflection.
Risk to inspect: reproduction at this downloaded resolution cannot prove small-type legibility or whether all visible designs are approved event assets.
Action: inventory originals, dimensions and approved designs; obtain rights and brand-owner clearance before turning these into a reusable merchandise family.

## 6. London-to-San Francisco learning check
Do not import London's grades or defect percentages into this event. The London audit is precedent only.
- Arrival/registration: P1/P2 show strong brand presence; registration-desk operation, queue flow and staffing are not covered.
- Upper-third logo placement: P4 merits sightline review; no aggregate blocking rate can be measured.
- Deep grounds under real lighting: P1/P3 merit lighting checks; no spectrophotometric colour comparison is available.
- Speaker-slide master: P4 looks coherent, but no delivered deck or other room photos establish adoption across speakers.
- Badge/lanyard improvements: no badge sample or clear name/lanyard photo; unassessed.
- Above-head wayfinding: arrival signs exist, but no corridor coverage or measured mounting heights; unassessed.
- Kiosks, lift wraps, pillars and screen surrounds: Element contains editable assets; a build-ready template is not evidence it was installed.
- QR/print QA: no original print sample, scan run, printer sign-off, mounting schedule or final vendor issue supplied; unassessed.

## 7. Operations, audience and business outcomes
A complete debrief needs reconciliation of registration versus check-in, session/room counts, staff feedback, accessibility, survey response denominators, sponsor deliverables and attributed lead progression. None was publicly verified in this review. Treat every field as awaiting owner evidence, not zero or successful by default.

For ROI, reconcile costs and attributable pipeline using an agreed reporting window. Distinguish sourced, influenced and converted opportunities. Do not imply causal revenue from social posts, visible crowd size or the advertised brand list.
